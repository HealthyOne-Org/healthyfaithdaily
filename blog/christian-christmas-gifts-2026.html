Christian Christmas Gifts 2026: Ideas for Every Bible Reader

November 13, 2026 · 8 min read · By Chris Hardaway
Photo via Pexels

Most Christian gifts end up in a drawer. A verse on a mug, a cross on a keychain, a devotional that gets opened twice in January. The gifts that last are the ones that put someone back into the Bible itself: a Bible they can actually read, a book that answers the question they keep asking, a journal that turns Sunday's sermon into Monday's prayer.

This guide is organized by person, because a good gift starts with who you are giving to, not with what is on sale. For each one you will find a few ideas and, more importantly, why it helps that person read Scripture. At the end is one gift anyone can give for free.

Why give a gift that points to Scripture?

Christmas is a season of gifts because it celebrates a gift. Paul ends a long section on generosity with one line of praise:

Thanks be unto God for his unspeakable gift.

2 Corinthians 9:15 (KJV)

The Greek word for "gift" there is dōrea (Strong's G1431), a free gift, given without being earned. "Unspeakable" is anekdiēgētos (G411), literally "that cannot be fully told." Christians have read the verse as pointing to Christ himself. James makes the same move from the other direction: "Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights" (James 1:17). He uses two different words, dosis (G1394), the act of giving, and dōrēma (G1434), the thing given. Both the giving and the gift come from God.

That gives Christmas shopping a simple test. Will this help the person I love know the Giver a little better? A Bible they will read passes. So does a book that answers an honest doubt, or a journal that helps them pray. A trinket usually does not, and that is fine; not every gift has to be spiritual. But if you want one under the tree that is, the ideas below are a good place to start.

Christian gifts for kids

Scripture expects children to learn it early. Paul reminded Timothy "that from a child thou hast known the holy scriptures, which are able to make thee wise unto salvation through faith which is in Christ Jesus" (2 Timothy 3:15). For small children, that starts with the story told simply and read aloud by someone they trust.

  • Babies and toddlers: a pop-up nativity board book. Sturdy pages they can lift and pull, and the Christmas story told in a few lines.
  • Preschool to early elementary: The Beginner's Bible. Short Bible stories with pictures, from Creation to the early church, at a length that fits bedtime.
  • Older kids: The Action Bible. The whole Bible story in comic-book form. It gives them the big story the rest of the Bible fits into.

One honest caution: story Bibles retell and simplify. They are a doorway, not a replacement. As a child grows, read the actual passage alongside the story so they learn where it lives in Scripture.

Christian gifts for teens

Teenagers are asking real questions, and they can tell when an adult is dodging them. The best gift for a teen respects that. Peter told believers to "be ready always to give an answer to every man that asketh you a reason of the hope that is in you with meekness and fear" (1 Peter 3:15). A teen who is wrestling deserves good answers, not slogans.

  • The Case for Christ by Lee Strobel. A journalist works through the historical evidence for Jesus by interviewing scholars. It reads like an investigation, which is why skeptical teens finish it.
  • Mere Christianity by C. S. Lewis. The classic case for the faith, written for honest doubters. Better for older teens who like to argue.
  • Scripture memory cards. Pocket cards with verses printed on them, for a phone case, a locker or a car visor. "Thy word have I hid in mine heart, that I might not sin against thee" (Psalm 119:11) is easier with something to hold.

Christian gifts for new believers

Someone who came to faith this year needs two things: a Bible they can find their way around, and help understanding what they read. Peter says new Christians should, "as newborn babes, desire the sincere milk of the word, that ye may grow thereby" (1 Peter 2:2).

  • A Bible with room to write: a KJV journaling Bible has wide margins for notes, prayers and cross-references right beside the text. New readers remember what they write down.
  • Bible book tabs. Small, but many new readers stall because they cannot find Habakkuk while everyone else already has.
  • Mere Christianity again, for the believer who wants to understand what they now believe and why.

Pair it with a simple plan. A new reader does better with one Gospel read slowly than with Genesis-to-Revelation in a year. Our Advent reading plan for 2026 is a good first month: one short passage a day from the prophets to the manger.

Christian gifts for men

Many men want to read the Bible daily and do not know where to start, or start strong and fade by February. Short and consistent beats long and occasional.

  • One Minute with God for Men. 365 one-page devotions. One page is a realistic goal for a man who has never kept a daily habit.
  • Strong's Exhaustive Concordance, for the man who already reads and wants to dig. It is the print reference behind the H and G numbers in every word study, and it rewards curiosity for decades.
  • The Ruthless Elimination of Hurry by John Mark Comer, on rest, Sabbath and slowing down enough to be with God. A fit for anyone running on empty.

Christian gifts for women

The same rule applies: give what fits the actual person, not the gift-aisle stereotype. Some women want a devotional, some want a concordance. A few ideas that have been popular this year:

  • A Beautiful Year with Jesus from Alabaster. A 365-day devotional that walks through the Bible a day at a time, a natural New Year's start.
  • A guided prayer journal. A place to write prayers down and come back to see how they were answered. Malachi 3:16 describes a "book of remembrance" written before the Lord for those who feared Him; a prayer journal is a small earthly echo of that.
  • Making Peace with What I Can't Control by Lysa TerKeurst, a new book on overthinking and trying to fix everything, for someone carrying a lot into the new year. If anxiety is part of their story, our list of Bible verses for anxiety is a good companion. (Books and verses help, and they are not a substitute for a doctor or counselor when worry is heavy.)

For the Bible reader who has everything

Some people already own three Bibles and a shelf of commentaries. They are the easiest to buy for, if you think in terms of tools rather than books. The Bereans "searched the scriptures daily" (Acts 17:11); give them something that helps them search.

  • No-bleed Bible highlighters. Gel highlighters that will not soak through thin Bible paper. Serious readers always need more.
  • A Bible journaling kit: pens, highlighters, tabs and stencils in one gift box. Pairs well with a journaling Bible.
  • A sermon notes journal, with prompted pages for the text, the main points and one thing to do this week. Our guide on how to take sermon notes explains how to use one well.

For grandparents and older readers

"And even to your old age I am he; and even to hoar hairs will I carry you" (Isaiah 46:4). Many older believers have read the Bible their whole lives and now struggle with small print. A KJV Giant Print Bible gives them large, easy-to-read King James text with thumb index tabs and a ribbon marker.

For the whole family this Advent

If you are buying before Advent begins on November 29, give something the family uses together. Moses told Israel to talk about God's words "when thou sittest in thine house, and when thou walkest by the way, and when thou liest down, and when thou risest up" (Deuteronomy 6:7).

  • An Advent wreath and candles: four candles for the four Sundays, plus a center candle for Christmas.
  • A family Advent devotional with short daily readings to go with the candle, written for children and parents together.

How to choose: three questions

  1. Where are they right now? A seeker needs answers, a new believer needs a readable Bible, a longtime reader needs tools. Match the gift to the season of their faith.
  2. Will they use it in January? The best Christian gift is the one still on the nightstand in March, not the one that looked nice under the tree.
  3. Can you add a note? Write one verse inside the cover and why you chose it for them. That handwritten line is often what they keep longest.

The wise men brought "gold, and frankincense, and myrrh" (Matthew 2:11), costly gifts chosen for who the Child was. The point was never the price. It was the worship. A gift that helps someone open the Word and meet that same Child is worth more than anything on a wish list.

FAQ

What is a good Christian Christmas gift?

The best Christian gifts help someone read and understand the Bible: a readable Bible (giant print for older readers, a journaling Bible for note-takers), a book that answers real questions, or a prayer journal. Match the gift to where the person is in their faith rather than picking a generic religious item.

What should I give a new Christian for Christmas?

A Bible they can find their way around, ideally with wide margins for notes, plus book tabs and a simple reading plan. Start them in one Gospel rather than at Genesis. A free Bible app like Faith Daily Bible on their phone helps them read on the go.

What is a good Christian gift for a teenager?

Something that takes their questions seriously. The Case for Christ and Mere Christianity are long-standing favorites with teens who are thinking hard about faith, and Scripture memory cards are a practical way to keep verses close. The Action Bible works well for younger teens.

Is there a free Christian gift I can give?

Yes. Help someone install a free Bible app and show them how to use it. Faith Daily Bible is free on iPhone and Android with 17 translations in English and Spanish, audio, a verse of the day and reading plans, with no ads and no account required.
