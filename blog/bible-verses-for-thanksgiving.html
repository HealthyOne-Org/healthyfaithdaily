Bible Verses for Thanksgiving: 20 KJV Verses and How to Pray Them

November 6, 2026 · 8 min read · By Chris Hardaway
Photo via Pexels

Thanksgiving is the one day of the year when a whole country is asked to say thank you at the same moment. For Christians it is also a chance to do on purpose what Scripture asks us to do every day. The Bible has a great deal to say about gratitude, and most of it is more honest than a greeting card.

Below are KJV verses for Thanksgiving grouped by the occasion you are actually in: around the table, after a hard year, with family (easy or not), and when you simply want to thank God for who He is. Each one has a short line on how to pray it.

What the Bible means by "thanksgiving"

Two Hebrew words carry most of the Old Testament's gratitude. The verb yadah (Strong's H3034) is usually translated "give thanks" or "praise," as in Psalm 136:1, "O give thanks unto the LORD." The noun todah (H8426) means thanksgiving, but it also names a specific sacrifice: the thank offering of Leviticus 7:12. So when Psalm 116:17 says "I will offer to thee the sacrifice of thanksgiving," the psalmist is describing something he brought to the altar, not only a feeling.

In the New Testament the Greek verb is eucharisteo (G2168), "to give thanks," and the noun eucharistia (G2169). Jesus "gave thanks" over the bread before feeding the five thousand and again at the Last Supper, which is why many churches call the Lord's Supper the Eucharist. Biblical gratitude, in other words, is not a mood. It is something you say and do, out loud, to Someone.

Around the table

1. Psalm 136:1. "O give thanks unto the LORD; for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever." The whole psalm repeats that last line 26 times, and it was likely sung as a call and response. How to pray it: have one person read each verse and let everyone at the table answer, "for his mercy endureth for ever."

2. Deuteronomy 8:10. "When thou hast eaten and art full, then thou shalt bless the LORD thy God for the good land which he hath given thee." Israel was told to give thanks after the meal, too, because a full stomach is when people forget. How to pray it: before the pie, pause once more: "We are full. Thank you."

3. Psalm 145:15–16. "The eyes of all wait upon thee; and thou givest them their meat in due season. Thou openest thine hand, and satisfiest the desire of every living thing." How to pray it: name the people whose hands brought the food here, the farmer, the driver, the cook, and thank God for His hand behind theirs.

4. 1 Timothy 4:4–5. "For every creature of God is good, and nothing to be refused, if it be received with thanksgiving: For it is sanctified by the word of God and prayer." How to pray it: a simple grace is enough. Thanksgiving is what turns a meal into a gift received.

5. Acts 2:46. The first believers were "breaking bread from house to house" and "did eat their meat with gladness and singleness of heart." How to pray it: ask for gladness and a single heart at this table, not a perfect one.

After a hard year

Some years you come to the table with an empty chair, a diagnosis, a job that ended, or a prayer that has not been answered. Scripture does not ask you to pretend. It shows people giving thanks with the loss still in view.

Although the fig tree shall not blossom, neither shall fruit be in the vines; the labour of the olive shall fail, and the fields shall yield no meat; the flock shall be cut off from the fold, and there shall be no herd in the stalls: Yet I will rejoice in the LORD, I will joy in the God of my salvation.

Habakkuk 3:17–18 (KJV)

6. Habakkuk 3:17–18. This is a harvest list with every item crossed out: no figs, no grapes, no olives, no crops, no flock, no herd. Then the word "yet." Habakkuk does not thank God for the failed harvest. He rejoices in God in spite of it. How to pray it: name honestly what did not come this year, then say your own "yet."

7. 1 Thessalonians 5:18. "In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you." Notice the preposition: in every thing, not necessarily for every thing. Ephesians 5:20 does say "giving thanks always for all things," and Christians have read that line in different ways, some broadly and some as "for all His gifts." How to pray it: "In this, I still thank you for ____."

8. Job 1:21. "The LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away; blessed be the name of the LORD." Job said this on the worst day of his life, and the rest of the book shows he also argued, wept and questioned. How to pray it: thank God for the years you had with what you lost.

9. Psalm 30:11–12. "Thou hast turned for me my mourning into dancing... O LORD my God, I will give thanks unto thee for ever." How to pray it: if the dancing has not come yet, pray it forward: "You turned it for David. Turn it for me."

10. Psalm 116:12, 17. "What shall I render unto the LORD for all his benefits toward me?... I will offer to thee the sacrifice of thanksgiving." In a hard year, gratitude can feel like a sacrifice. The psalm calls it one. How to pray it: offer one sentence of thanks that costs you something to say.

Gratitude in Scripture is not denial. Habakkuk, Job and the writer of Psalm 116 all gave thanks with the loss still in view. If this year has left you grieving or worn down, Scripture is for the soul, not a substitute for care: a pastor, a counselor or a doctor can walk with you too. With family, easy or not

11. Philippians 1:3. "I thank my God upon every remembrance of you." Paul wrote this from custody to a church that had sent him help. How to pray it: go around the table and finish the sentence for the person on your left: "I thank God for you because..."

12. Psalm 133:1. "Behold, how good and how pleasant it is for brethren to dwell together in unity!" How to pray it: thank God for the unity you do have, even if it is only around this one meal.

13. Romans 12:18. "If it be possible, as much as lieth in you, live peaceably with all men." Paul's two conditions, "if it be possible" and "as much as lieth in you," admit that peace is not always in your hands. How to pray it: before you walk in the door, ask for patience for your part of the conversation and leave the rest to God.

14. Colossians 3:15. "And let the peace of God rule in your hearts, to the which also ye are called in one body; and be ye thankful." Peace and thankfulness are put side by side. How to pray it: "Let your peace be the umpire in me today."

15. Psalm 78:4. "We will not hide them from their children, shewing to the generation to come the praises of the LORD." How to pray it: tell the kids at the table one specific thing God did for your family this year. That is what this verse asks for.

Thanking God for who He is

16. Psalm 100:4. "Enter into his gates with thanksgiving, and into his courts with praise: be thankful unto him, and bless his name." In the Hebrew heading Psalm 100 is a psalm "for thanksgiving" (todah); the KJV renders the title "A Psalm of praise." How to pray it: start your prayer with thanks before you ask for anything.

17. Psalm 103:2. "Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits." David talks to his own soul here. Forgetting is the default. How to pray it: list five benefits from this year, out loud or on paper, before the day is over.

18. James 1:17. "Every good gift and every perfect gift is from above, and cometh down from the Father of lights, with whom is no variableness, neither shadow of turning." How to pray it: trace one good thing on the table back to its Giver.

19. Luke 17:15–16. Ten lepers were healed. One "turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God, And fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan." How to pray it: be the one who comes back. Thank God for a prayer He already answered.

20. 2 Corinthians 9:15. "Thanks be unto God for his unspeakable gift." Paul ends a long discussion of generosity by pointing to the gift behind all others, Christ Himself. How to pray it: close the day with this verse as your whole prayer.

How to use these verses on Thanksgiving Day

  • Pick one for the table. Psalm 136 read responsively works with any age. Psalm 100 is short enough for a nervous reader.
  • Read the psalm, not only the verse. Many thanksgiving psalms are testimonies. Psalm 66, which is marked several times with selah, says "Come and hear, all ye that fear God, and I will declare what he hath done for my soul" (66:16).
  • Write a list. Psalm 103:2 says "forget not." A written list of the year's mercies is something you can read again next November.
  • Keep the hard verses close. If this year was heavy, the section above pairs well with our Bible verses for anxiety, grouped the same way by what you feel.

FAQ

What is a good Bible verse to read at Thanksgiving dinner?

Psalm 136:1, "O give thanks unto the LORD; for he is good: for his mercy endureth for ever," is a natural fit, and the whole psalm can be read responsively with everyone answering "for his mercy endureth for ever." Psalm 100 is a short alternative, and Deuteronomy 8:10 is a good reminder to give thanks after the meal too.

What does the Bible say about being thankful in hard times?

1 Thessalonians 5:18 says "In every thing give thanks," which most readers take to mean giving thanks in every situation rather than calling every situation good. Habakkuk 3:17-18 and Job 1:21 show people thanking God while naming real loss, and the Psalms place thanksgiving and lament side by side.

What is the Hebrew word for thanksgiving?

The noun is todah (Strong's H8426), which means thanksgiving and also names the thank offering in Leviticus 7:12. The related verb yadah (H3034) is translated "give thanks" or "praise," as in Psalm 136:1. In the New Testament the Greek verb is eucharisteo (G2168).

Is Thanksgiving a biblical holiday?

No. Thanksgiving Day is a national holiday, not one commanded in Scripture. But the Bible does command regular thanksgiving, and Israel's feasts, especially the Feast of Tabernacles at the end of the harvest, were times of thanks to God for provision. Many Christians use the holiday as an occasion to practice that biblical habit.

The bottom line

The Bible's gratitude is not reserved for good years. It is said at full tables and failed harvests, with family close and far away. Pick one verse from the section that fits your Thanksgiving, read it aloud, pray it in your own words, and write down what God has done so you can remember it next year.
