25 Bible Verses for Anxiety, and How to Pray Each One (2026)

October 29, 2026 · 8 min read · By Chris Hardaway
Photo via Pexels

Anxiety does not wait for a convenient time. It shows up at 2 a.m., in the car before a meeting, in the quiet after the kids are asleep. The Bible does not pretend otherwise. It never tells anxious people that they have no reason to be afraid. It tells them who is with them. That is a different kind of comfort, and it holds.

Below are 25 verses for anxiety, grouped by what you are actually feeling, because "I'm anxious" usually means something more specific: I can't stop my thoughts, I feel alone in this, I'm afraid of what's coming, I'm exhausted. Each verse has one line on how to pray it. Read slowly. One verse, prayed, is worth more than twenty-five skimmed.

A note before you start: Scripture is for the soul, not a substitute for care. If anxiety is keeping you from sleeping, working or being with people, talk to a doctor or a counselor. Prayer and treatment are not rivals. Many of the people in these verses had both God and a community around them.

When you can't stop your thoughts

Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding, shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.

Philippians 4:6–7 (KJV)

1. Philippians 4:6–7. Paul gives a sequence: pray, ask, give thanks. Then the peace comes, and it "keeps" your mind the way a guard keeps a gate. How to pray it: name the thing you are anxious about out loud, ask for exactly what you want, then thank God for one thing. Do all three.

2. Isaiah 26:3. "Thou wilt keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on thee." The Hebrew behind "stayed" means leaned on, propped up. How to pray it: "Lord, I'm leaning my mind on you, because it won't hold itself up tonight."

3. 2 Corinthians 10:5. "Bringing into captivity every thought to the obedience of Christ." Anxious thoughts are not neutral; they make claims. This verse gives you permission to interrogate them. How to pray it: take the worst thought and ask, "Is this true? What does Christ say about it?"

4. Psalm 94:19. "In the multitude of my thoughts within me thy comforts delight my soul." The psalmist's thoughts are a multitude, a crowd. God's comfort is what delights him anyway. How to pray it: "My thoughts are a crowd today. Be louder than the crowd."

5. Romans 8:6. "To be spiritually minded is life and peace." How to pray it: ask for your mind to be set on one true thing about God for the next ten minutes.

When you feel alone in it

6. Deuteronomy 31:8. "He it is that doth go before thee; he will be with thee, he will not fail thee, neither forsake thee: fear not, neither be dismayed." Moses says this to Joshua, who is about to take a job he did not ask for. How to pray it: "You go before me into tomorrow. I'll follow."

7. Psalm 23:4. "Though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me." Notice it says through. The valley is not the destination. How to pray it: "I'm in the valley. You're with me. Walk me through."

8. Isaiah 41:10. "Fear thou not; for I am with thee: be not dismayed; for I am thy God: I will strengthen thee; yea, I will help thee; yea, I will uphold thee with the right hand of my righteousness." Five promises in one verse. How to pray it: read it with your name in place of "thee."

9. Matthew 28:20. "Lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world." The last words of Matthew's Gospel. How to pray it: "You said always. I'm holding you to it today."

10. Hebrews 13:5–6. "I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee. So that we may boldly say, The Lord is my helper, and I will not fear what man shall do unto me." How to pray it: say the second half out loud. Boldly is the instruction.

When you're afraid of what's coming

11. Matthew 6:34. "Take therefore no thought for the morrow: for the morrow shall take thought for the things of itself. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof." Jesus is not saying tomorrow will be fine. He is saying you only have to carry today. How to pray it: "Give me what I need for today. Just today."

12. Psalm 56:3. "What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee." Not if I am afraid. When. David assumes fear will come and decides in advance what he will do with it. How to pray it: make it your plan: "When I'm afraid today, I'll trust you."

13. Jeremiah 29:11. "For I know the thoughts that I think toward you, saith the LORD, thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you an expected end." This was written to people facing seventy years of exile, so it is not a promise that things get easy fast. It is a promise that God's intentions toward you are good. How to pray it: "Your thoughts toward me are peace. Help me believe that about this situation."

14. Proverbs 3:5–6. "Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths." How to pray it: "I don't understand this. I'm choosing to trust you instead of my understanding."

15. Psalm 46:1–2. "God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Therefore will not we fear, though the earth be removed." How to pray it: "You're a very present help. Be present now." Psalm 46 also marks three places to stop with the word Selah; here is what Selah means and why those pauses matter.

16. John 14:27. "Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you: not as the world giveth, give I unto you. Let not your heart be troubled, neither let it be afraid." How to pray it: "Your peace, not the world's version. I'll take yours."

When you're exhausted

17. Matthew 11:28–30. "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest." Jesus does not say "try harder." He says come. How to pray it: "I'm heavy laden. I'm coming. I need the rest you promised."

18. Psalm 55:22. "Cast thy burden upon the LORD, and he shall sustain thee." The word for "burden" can mean what has been given to you, your lot. How to pray it: picture the specific thing, and hand it over. Then stop picking it back up.

19. 1 Peter 5:7. "Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you." Note the reason: because he cares. Not because you have to. How to pray it: "You care about this more than I do. I'm giving it to you."

20. Isaiah 40:31. "They that wait upon the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings as eagles; they shall run, and not be weary; and they shall walk, and not faint." How to pray it: "I can't run today. Help me walk and not faint."

21. Psalm 4:8. "I will both lay me down in peace, and sleep: for thou, LORD, only makest me dwell in safety." A bedtime verse, written by a man with real enemies. How to pray it: say it at the edge of the bed before you lie down.

When you need to be reminded who God is

22. Psalm 34:4. "I sought the LORD, and he heard me, and delivered me from all my fears." How to pray it: "I'm seeking you. Hear me. Deliver me from this fear."

23. 2 Timothy 1:7. "For God hath not given us the spirit of fear; but of power, and of love, and of a sound mind." The fear you feel is real, but it is not from God, and he has given you something to replace it with. How to pray it: ask for the three things named: power, love, a sound mind.

24. Romans 8:38–39. "Neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come... shall be able to separate us from the love of God." Things to come is on the list. Your worst-case scenario is on the list. How to pray it: "Even the thing I'm afraid of can't separate me from your love."

25. Lamentations 3:22–23. "It is of the LORD's mercies that we are not consumed, because his compassions fail not. They are new every morning: great is thy faithfulness." Written in the rubble of Jerusalem. How to pray it: first thing tomorrow, before your phone: "New mercies. Thank you."

How to actually use these verses

A list is a starting point, not a practice. Here is what works for people who use Scripture against anxiety and keep using it:

  • Pick one, not twenty-five. Choose the section that matches what you feel today and take one verse from it. Write it on a card or set it as your lock screen.
  • Read it in context. Open the chapter. Philippians 4 was written from prison. Psalm 56 was written while David was captured by the Philistines. Knowing who said it, and when, is what makes the verse credible when your anxiety says it is not.
  • Look at one word. "Stayed" in Isaiah 26:3, "cast" in Psalm 55:22, "keep" in Philippians 4:7. The original Hebrew and Greek words are usually more concrete than the English, and the picture they give you sticks.
  • Pray it, don't just read it. The "how to pray it" lines above are a start. Turn the verse into a sentence you say to God in the first person.
  • Come back tomorrow. Anxiety is rarely beaten in one sitting. The mercies are new every morning for a reason.

FAQ

What is the most comforting Bible verse for anxiety?

Philippians 4:6-7 is the one most people return to, because it gives both an instruction (pray about everything, with thanksgiving) and a promise (the peace of God will keep your heart and mind). Isaiah 26:3 and Psalm 94:19 are close behind for people whose anxiety is mostly racing thoughts.

Does the Bible say anxiety is a sin?

The Bible treats anxiety as a human condition to bring to God, not a charge to be convicted of. Jesus says "take no thought for the morrow" as an invitation to trust, and Paul says "be careful for nothing" in the same breath as telling people how to pray. Feeling anxious is not a failure. Leaving God out of it is what Scripture warns against.

How do I pray when I'm too anxious to focus?

Use one verse as the prayer. Say Psalm 56:3 out loud: "What time I am afraid, I will trust in thee." Repeat it slowly. Short, borrowed prayers are how the Psalms were used for centuries, and they work precisely when you cannot find your own words.

Should I read these verses instead of seeing a counselor?

No. Read them alongside whatever care you need. Scripture speaks to the heart; a doctor or counselor can help with sleep, panic and the physical side of anxiety. Many believers do both, and nothing in the Bible asks you to choose.

The bottom line

Anxiety tells you that you are alone and that the worst is certain. These 25 verses answer both claims, not by promising an easy outcome but by promising a present God. Pick one that matches what you feel today, read it in context, pray it in your own words, and come back tomorrow for new mercies.
